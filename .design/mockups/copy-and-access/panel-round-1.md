Three of four pick B for the whole app; all four want the full list of words one tap away. All four say C's always-visible sentence would get in the way once they know the word (Bia included, who picked it for a first visit).

## Where they agree

1. **B's sentence about the player's own data** is what answers the worry (Caio, Lucas, Marta): "continua salvo com as 12 cartas", "Este deck do Pedro é de Classic Constructed".
2. **A dotted underline does not look tappable on a phone** (Lucas, Marta), and on a menu option it looks like the option itself (Caio: "tocar nela aposenta o deck sem eu querer").
3. **Words the explanations use without explaining them**: "recurso" and "custo" in the pitch sentence (Bia, Marta, Caio), "herói adulto" and "formato" in the Classic Constructed sentence (Bia, Caio, Marta).
4. **The deck stages are not explained** except "Aposentado" (Bia, Caio, Lucas): "Ideia", "Montando", "Pronto", "Jogando".

## Problems found by one persona, worth keeping

- "(Vermelho)" in a card name needs its "?" too, on a page a parent reads (Marta): without it the color reads as decoration and she could buy the wrong card.
- "Faltam 5 cartas" against "falta 1" on the same page: cards or copies? (Marta).
- "Aposentado" is the deck's stage; a returning player first reads it as "my hero was retired" (Lucas).
- What a format means for the buyer: "o Pedro precisa de 3 de cada?" (Marta).
- Card types are not explained: "Instantâneo", "Reação de ataque", "Reação de defesa", "Equipamento" (Bia); nor "Foil" (Bia), "Alternativas", "Indisponível na Cúpula DT" (Caio, Lucas).
- Lucas expected Silver Age to be named as what replaced an older format; that claim is from his profile and was not checked.

## Asked for, outside this journey

- The card text in English ("go again", "Temper") (Marta): card text stays in English by design; the comprehensive rules' glossary, already indexed for the Tutor, could explain keywords later.
- "[CC/GOAL]" in a deck name (Lucas): a name the sharer chose on Fabrary.

- Caio and Lucas know most of the words, so their picks weigh what gets in the way of someone who knows.
